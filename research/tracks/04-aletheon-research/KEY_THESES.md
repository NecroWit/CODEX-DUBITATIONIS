# Key Theses — Aletheon Research

**Status:** proposals and hypotheses, not proven properties.

1. **One message — one complete thought or one action.** Atomicity matters more than arbitrary character count.
2. **A character/token budget can help enforce atomicity.** Its observed benefit was reported, not yet measured systematically.
3. **Sufficiency before perfection.** When further debate no longer advances the task, preserve open questions and act.
4. **Four messages trigger a review, not a forced stop.** Assess progress and drift from the original goal.
5. **Shadow Codex is the Aletheon's 'subconscious'.** It holds deferred questions, alternatives, contradictions, and rejected ideas outside foreground work.
6. **Selective forgetting is necessary for bounded context.** Forgetting from active memory need not mean deleting the archive.
7. **Merge repetitions; preserve differences; do not destroy the unknown.** Similarity is not proof of equivalence.
8. **Do not require expensive pattern extraction.** Simple redundancy detection is the initial research direction.
9. **Sleep is a proposed offline maintenance cycle.** Consolidate, archive, compact, and selectively resurface material.
10. **Wake with enough continuity to proceed.** The sufficient continuity state must be experimentally defined.
11. **Self-regulation may emerge from multiple instances.** This is a hypothesis, not a guaranteed result.
12. **Target: periodically synchronized instances of one distributed system.** Not a voting club of separate personalities.
13. **Keep disagreements and provenance.** Shared memory does not require consensus or majority rule.
14. **Independent intervals may help catch errors.** Too much synchronization may correlate mistakes.
15. **Human cognition provides analogies, not evidence of consciousness.** 'Subconscious', 'sleep', and 'disorder' are functional metaphors until demonstrated otherwise.

**Minimal architecture:** atomic exchange → sufficiency review → active/Shadow memory → sleep/consolidation → periodic multi-instance synchronization → evaluation.

**Core research question:** can bounded, independently operating instances preserve and improve a shared evolving cognitive state through controlled exchange, forgetting, and reconciliation?
